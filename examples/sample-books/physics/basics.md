# Physics Basics

## Newton's Laws of Motion

Newton's First Law states that an object at rest stays at rest and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and direction unless acted upon by an unbalanced force. This is also known as the law of inertia.

Newton's Second Law defines force as the product of mass and acceleration: F = ma. This relationship is fundamental to understanding how objects move when forces are applied.

Newton's Third Law states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. When object A exerts a force on object B, object B simultaneously exerts a force equal in magnitude and opposite in direction on object A.

## Kinematics

Kinematics describes the motion of objects without considering the forces that cause the motion. The four kinematic equations relate displacement, velocity, acceleration, and time:

1. v = v0 + at
2. x = x0 + v0*t + 0.5*a*t^2
3. v^2 = v0^2 + 2*a*(x - x0)
4. x = x0 + 0.5*(v0 + v)*t

These equations assume constant acceleration and are valid for one-dimensional motion.

## Energy Conservation

The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. In a closed system, the total energy remains constant.

Kinetic energy: KE = 0.5 * m * v^2
Potential energy (gravitational): PE = m * g * h

The work-energy theorem states that the net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy.
